# N1 Vocabulary — Nuance & Example Sentences

===word: 代わり番===
Nuance: 代わり番 refers to taking turns or alternating roles in a cooperative context. It carries a neutral, everyday register and is often used when two or more people share responsibility or perform an action in sequence, like rotating shifts or trading off duties. Unlike 交代, which is more formal and often used in workplace scheduling, 代わり番 feels slightly more conversational and can even imply a playful or informal arrangement.
Example1: 代わり番でピアノを弾いて、一曲ずつ披露した。
Example1_EN: We took turns playing the piano, each performing one song.
Example2: 子どもの世話は妻と代わり番でやっている。
Example2_EN: My wife and I take turns looking after the children.
Example3: 代わり番に質問に答えていったので、全員が発言できた。
Example3_EN: Since we answered the questions in turn, everyone got a chance to speak.

===word: 槍玉に挙げる===
Nuance: 槍玉に挙げる means to single someone out as a target of criticism or blame, often in a public or group setting. The image is of holding someone up as a "spearhead" for attack — making them a scapegoat or an example to be denounced. It carries a distinctly negative connotation and is typically used in contexts of media criticism, workplace blame, or social targeting, where one person bears the brunt of collective disapproval.
Example1: 部長はミスの責任をすべて新人の課長に槍玉に挙げた。
Example1_EN: The department head made the new section chief the scapegoat for all the mistakes.
Example2: マスコミはまたあの政治家を槍玉に挙げて叩いている。
Example2_EN: The media is once again ganging up on that politician and tearing him apart.
Example3: なぜいつも私だけが槍玉に挙げられるのか理解できない。
Example3_EN: I can't understand why I'm always the one singled out for criticism.

===word: 無双===
Nuance: 無双 describes something that is peerless or unmatched in its category, carrying a strong sense of being the absolute best with no equal. It can be used both literally — for historical warriors or martial arts masters — and figuratively, for skills, products, or performances that stand head and shoulders above the competition. The word has a dramatic, almost epic tone and is commonly found in game titles, anime, and celebratory contexts, so it feels slightly hyperbolic in everyday speech.
Example1: 彼の剣技はまさに当代無双の境地に達していた。
Example1_EN: His swordsmanship had reached a truly peerless level unmatched in the current era.
Example2: このゲームでは無双の武将を操作して敵をなぎ倒す。
Example2_EN: In this game, you control an unparalleled warrior and mow down enemies.
Example3: 彼女のピアノの腕前はコンクールで無双だった。
Example3_EN: Her piano skills were unmatched at the competition.

===word: 隔靴掻痒===
Nuance: 隔靴掻痒 describes the frustrating feeling of being unable to get complete satisfaction or resolution, like trying to scratch an itch through the sole of your boot. It is used when an explanation is too vague, a solution is only partial, or a performance falls just short of hitting the mark. The register is literary and somewhat formal, making it more common in written commentary, reviews, and critiques than in casual conversation.
Example1: 彼の説明はいつも隔靴掻痒の感があり、核心を突かない。
Example1_EN: His explanations always leave you feeling unsatisfied, never quite hitting the core issue.
Example2: この小説の結末は隔靴掻痒で、読後感がすっきりしない。
Example2_EN: The ending of this novel is frustratingly unsatisfying, leaving a bad aftertaste.
Example3: 対策が中途半端で、隔靴掻痒の状態が続いている。
Example3_EN: The countermeasures are half-baked, so the frustrating state of limbo continues.

===word: 水揚げ===
Nuance: 水揚げ refers to the landing or unloading of a catch of fish (or other goods from a ship), and by extension the total haul itself. In the context of a massive tuna auction at Tsukiji or Toyosu, it carries a weighty, commercial feel — the scale and value of the catch are implied. It can also be used metaphorically for the amount of money or revenue "brought in" from a venture, particularly in the entertainment industry when referring to an actress's earnings.
Example1: 今朝のマグロの水揚げは過去最高の記録を更新した。
Example1_EN: This morning's tuna haul broke the all-time record.
Example2: 台風の影響で漁船が港に戻れず、水揚げが大幅に減少した。
Example2_EN: Due to the typhoon, fishing boats couldn't return to port, and the catch decreased sharply.
Example3: 彼女は今月の水揚げがトップだと聞いて、にっこり笑った。
Example3_EN: When she heard her revenue this month was the highest, she smiled.

===word: 手を焼く===
Nuance: 手を焼く means to have one's hands full dealing with someone or something troublesome, often to the point of exasperation. It implies that the person or situation is beyond easy handling and requires constant, draining effort. It is used in casual to semi-formal speech about troublesome students, difficult clients, rebellious children, or any ongoing problem that resists solution.
Example1: あのクラスの生徒たちのやんちゃぶりには、どの先生も手を焼いている。
Example1_EN: Every teacher has their hands full with the unruly behavior of the students in that class.
Example2: 新しい上司の細かい指示の出し方には本当に手を焼いている。
Example2_EN: I'm really struggling with our new boss's habit of giving overly detailed instructions.
Example3: この古いソフトウェアのバグには手を焼いてきたが、やっと対策が見つかった。
Example3_EN: I've had my hands full with the bugs in this old software, but I've finally found a fix.

===word: 露見する===
Nuance: 露見する means for a secret, hidden fact, or scandal to be exposed or discovered, often against the wishes of those involved. It carries a strong sense of something hidden coming to light, usually with negative consequences — a lie, a crime, a concealed relationship, or an embarrassing truth. The word is formal and slightly dramatic, common in news reports, crime fiction, and serious discussions, rather than lighthearted conversation.
Example1: 長年隠してきた不正会計がついに露見した。
Example1_EN: The accounting fraud that had been hidden for years was finally exposed.
Example2: 彼の二重生活は妻に露見して、離婚騒動に発展した。
Example2_EN: His double life was discovered by his wife and escalated into a divorce scandal.
Example3: 偽造書類を使っていることが露見しないように、細心の注意を払った。
Example3_EN: He took extreme care to ensure that his use of forged documents wouldn't be discovered.

===word: 他力本願===
Nuance: 他力本願 originally comes from Pure Land Buddhism, where it means relying on the power of Amida Buddha for salvation rather than one's own efforts. In modern usage, it carries a distinctly negative connotation of depending on others to do the work for you — a "let someone else handle it" attitude. It is used critically to describe people who expect easy solutions without putting in effort, or organizations that pass the buck rather than taking responsibility.
Example1: 彼はいつも他力本願で、自分では何もやろうとしない。
Example1_EN: He always expects others to do things for him and never tries to do anything himself.
Example2: 受験勉強を他力本願で考えているようでは、合格は難しい。
Example2_EN: If you're expecting to pass the exam just by relying on others, it's going to be tough.
Example3: 他力本願の姿勢では、このプロジェクトは成功しないだろう。
Example3_EN: With this kind of dependence-on-others attitude, this project won't succeed.

===word: 沽券にかかわる===
Nuance: 沽券にかかわる means that something affects one's honor, dignity, or professional pride — it is a matter of personal standing. The word 沽券 originally referred to a license or deed, and by extension one's social value or reputation. This expression is used when a situation is so beneath one's dignity or so damaging to one's reputation that it cannot be ignored or tolerated. It carries a somewhat old-fashioned, masculine tone and is often used in business or hierarchical contexts.
Example1: ベテランの職人として、こんな粗雑な仕事を納品するのは沽券にかかわる。
Example1_EN: As a veteran craftsman, delivering such shoddy work would be a matter of professional honor.
Example2: 彼に負けを認めるのは沽券にかかわるので、最後まで譲らなかった。
Example2_EN: Admitting defeat to him would affect his dignity, so he wouldn't back down until the very end.
Example3: 上司の前でそんな無能ぶりを見せるのは沽券にかかわる。
Example3_EN: Showing such incompetence in front of my boss would be a blow to my pride.

===word: 名を挙げる===
Nuance: 名を挙げる means to make a name for oneself or to achieve distinction in a particular field. It implies active effort to gain recognition and respect, often through notable accomplishments, discoveries, or victories. While it can be used in ordinary contexts, it has a slightly ambitious and aspirational tone — the person is not just succeeding but doing so in a way that their name becomes widely known. It is more formal and literary than similar phrases like 有名になる.
Example1: 彼は若くして学会で名を挙げ、教授に抜擢された。
Example1_EN: He made a name for himself in academic circles at a young age and was promoted to professor.
Example2: オリンピックで金メダルを取り、一躍世界に名を挙げた。
Example2_EN: He won a gold medal at the Olympics and suddenly made a name for himself worldwide.
Example3: 父は一代でこの町で名を挙げた立志伝中の人物だ。
Example3_EN: My father is a self-made man who built his reputation in this town through his own efforts.

===word: 狐疑逡巡===
Nuance: 狐疑逡巡 describes a state of excessive hesitation and indecision, where one is so wracked by doubt that they cannot take action. The first part, 狐疑, literally means "fox-like suspicion" — the idea of being as wary and distrustful as a fox — and 逡巡 means to shrink back or waver. It is a four-character compound with a formal, literary register, used to describe someone who is paralyzed by overthinking in critical situations, often with a slightly critical tone.
Example1: 彼は重要な決断を前に狐疑逡巡して、チャンスを逃してしまった。
Example1_EN: Faced with an important decision, he was paralyzed by doubt and let the opportunity slip away.
Example2: 狐疑逡巡している暇はない。今すぐ行動しなければ。
Example2_EN: There's no time for hesitation and doubt. We have to act now.
Example3: 投資家たちは狐疑逡巡の末、結局その案件を見送った。
Example3_EN: After much hesitation and uncertainty, the investors ultimately decided to pass on the deal.

===word: 呼吸を合わせる===
Nuance: 呼吸を合わせる means to synchronize one's rhythm or timing with someone else, whether in a physical activity like sports or music, or in a collaborative work setting. It is about achieving a默契 — a wordless默契 where each person instinctively knows what the other will do. The nuance is almost always positive, implying teamwork, harmony, and mutual understanding. It is used in both literal and figurative contexts, from dance partners to business teams.
Example1: 新人は先輩と呼吸を合わせるのに苦労していた。
Example1_EN: The newcomer was struggling to get in sync with the senior colleague.
Example2: この曲はデュエットなので、お互いの呼吸を合わせることが何より大切だ。
Example2_EN: Since this is a duet, synchronizing our timing with each other is the most important thing.
Example3: 長年のコンビだから、呼吸を合わせるのに言葉はいらない。
Example3_EN: We've been a team for so long that we don't need words to get in sync.

===word: 布石===
Nuance: 布石 is a Go term that literally means "placing stones" as a preparatory move, and in broader usage it refers to any preliminary step or groundwork laid in advance to achieve a future goal. It carries a strategic, forward-thinking connotation — the payoff is not immediate, but the move sets up a favorable position down the line. It is used in business, career planning, negotiations, and any context where long-term positioning matters, and it has a neutral to positive tone.
Example1: 今回の海外出張は、新規市場開拓の布石となるはずだ。
Example1_EN: This overseas business trip should serve as a foundation for developing new markets.
Example2: 彼は政界進出の布石として、地元の有力者と関係を築き始めた。
Example2_EN: As a preliminary step toward entering politics, he began building relationships with local influential figures.
Example3: その提携は将来の大型プロジェクトに向けた重要な布石だった。
Example3_EN: That partnership was an important strategic move toward a future large-scale project.

===word: 縦横無尽===
Nuance: 縦横無尽 describes moving or acting freely and without restriction, in all directions, as if unimpeded by any obstacle. It combines 縦 (vertical) and 横 (horizontal) with 無尽 (inexhaustible), painting a picture of boundless, unrestricted movement. It is used for someone who navigates a field or domain with complete freedom and skill — a brilliant debater who can argue any point, a traveler who roams everywhere, or a leader who maneuvers through any situation with ease.
Example1: 彼は会議で縦横無尽に論を展開し、相手を圧倒した。
Example1_EN: He developed his argument freely and comprehensively in the meeting, overwhelming his opponents.
Example2: この作家は縦横無尽な想像力で、独創的な世界を築き上げた。
Example2_EN: This writer built an original world with an unrestrained, boundless imagination.
Example3: 彼女は政界を縦横無尽に駆け回り、誰もが一目置く存在になった。
Example3_EN: She moved through the political world with complete freedom, becoming someone everyone respected.

===word: 一網打尽===
Nuance: 一網打尽 means to capture or round up everything (or everyone) in one fell swoop, like catching all the fish in a single cast of the net. It is used when authorities arrest an entire criminal ring at once, when a team sweeps all the awards, or when a single action eliminates all problems simultaneously. The tone is dramatic and triumphant, often appearing in news headlines about police raids or sports victories. It implies efficiency and completeness.
Example1: 警察は暴力団事務所を一網打尽にし、幹部全員を逮捕した。
Example1_EN: The police raided the gang's office in a clean sweep and arrested all the senior members.

Example2_EN: The strategy is to wipe out all competing companies in one fell swoop with this campaign.
Example3: 彼女は主要な文学賞を一網打尽にして、文壇の話題をさらった。
Example3_EN: She swept all the major literary awards, becoming the talk of the literary world.

===word: 雲散霧消===
Nuance: 雲散霧消 describes something disappearing completely and without a trace, like clouds scattering or mist vanishing. It is used for abstract things — doubts, worries, suspicions, or rumors — that suddenly dissolve, leaving no residue. The tone is poetic and slightly dramatic, with a visual quality that makes it more vivid than plain words like 消える. It often implies that the disappearance brings relief or resolution, though it can also describe something valuable that has vanished irretrievably.
Example1: 彼の説明を聞いて、私の疑念は雲散霧消した。
Example1_EN: After hearing his explanation, my doubts vanished without a trace.

Example2_EN: Years of effort disappeared in an instant because of a single mistake.
Example3: その噂はすぐに雲散霧消し、誰も話題にしなくなった。
Example3_EN: The rumor quickly dissipated, and no one talked about it anymore.

===word: 四分五裂===
Nuance: 四分五裂 describes a state of complete disunity or fragmentation, where a group, organization, or country has broken into warring factions with no cohesion. The literal meaning is "split into four parts and five cracks" — total disintegration. It carries a strongly negative tone, used to describe political parties in turmoil, teams that cannot cooperate, or families torn apart by conflict. It is a dramatic, somewhat formal expression common in news analysis and historical commentary.
Example1: 与党は内部対立で四分五裂の状態に陥っている。
Example1_EN: The ruling party has fallen into a state of utter disunity due to internal conflict.

Example3_EN: If the team is in complete disunity, you can't win even winnable games.

===word: 東奔西走===
Nuance: 東奔西走 means running around busily in all directions, dealing with various tasks or obligations. The literal image is "running east and west" — a vivid picture of someone constantly in motion, shuttling between places or people. It carries a tone of busyness and dedication, but also hints at exhaustion or being overworked. It is used in both formal and informal contexts, from describing a salesperson's daily routine to a politician's campaign schedule.
Example1: 彼は選挙運動で東奔西走の毎日を送っている。

===word: 臨機応変===
Nuance: 臨機応変 describes the ability to adapt flexibly to changing circumstances, handling each situation as it comes rather than sticking rigidly to a plan. It is a highly positive quality in Japanese culture, where the ideal is to read the situation and respond appropriately. It is used in professional contexts (praise for a manager who handles a crisis well), in social settings (someone who can chat with anyone), and in self-introductions (as a strength in interviews). It implies both intelligence and social awareness.
Example1: 彼は臨機応変な対応ができるので、どんなトラブルでも頼りになる。

===word: 濫觴===
Nuance: 濫觴 literally means the source of a river, but is used metaphorically to mean the origin or beginning of something. The imagery is elegant: a river starts as a tiny stream no wider than a drinking cup (觴), yet eventually becomes a great flow. It is a highly literary and formal word, found in academic writing, historical essays, and formal speeches, rather than everyday conversation. It carries a sense of tracing something back to its humble or momentous beginnings.
Example1: 日本の近代小説の濫觴は、明治時代の言文一致運動にあると言われている。
Example1_EN: The origin of the modern Japanese novel is said to lie in the Meiji-era unification of speech and writing.

Example2_EN: This research institute is known as the birthplace of AI research in Japan.
Example3: 彼の作品は、その後のサブカルチャーの濫觴となった。
Example3_EN: His work became the origin of the subculture that followed.

===word: 不撓不屈===
Nuance: 不撓不屈 describes an indomitable spirit that never bends or breaks, no matter what obstacles or hardships arise. It is composed of two negated verbs — 不撓 (not bending) and 不屈 (not yielding) — doubling down on the idea of unwavering resolve. This is a highly positive, almost heroic quality, used to praise athletes, pioneers, historical figures, or anyone who perseveres against overwhelming odds. The register is formal and inspirational, often found in speeches, biographies, and commendations.
Example1: 彼は不撓不屈の精神で難病を克服し、再び舞台に立った。

===word: 切磋琢磨===
Nuance: 切磋琢磨 originally refers to the polishing and carving of bone, ivory, jade, and stone — a metaphor for the refining process. In modern usage, it means mutual improvement through friendly rivalry, where people spur each other on to grow and excel. The nuance is positive and collaborative: the focus is on both parties pushing each other to become better, not on defeating the other. It is used in contexts of students, athletes, artists, and colleagues who grow together through competition and mutual encouragement.

===word: 因果応報===
Nuance: 因果応報 encapsulates the Buddhist concept of karma — that one reaps what one sows, and that good or bad actions will eventually bring corresponding results. It is most often used in a negative sense, observing that someone's wrongdoing has finally caught up with them, like a fraudster being arrested or a bully facing consequences. The tone is moralistic and can be either neutral (as a philosophical observation) or vindicated (when someone "had it coming"). It is a common phrase in drama, news commentary, and everyday moral reflection.
Example1: 長年人を騙してきた彼がついに捕まったのは、因果応報だ。

===word: 泰然自若===
Nuance: 泰然自若 describes a state of complete composure and calm, remaining unshaken even in the face of crisis or pressure. The image is of someone sitting serenely while the world around them is in chaos. It is a highly positive quality in Japanese culture, associated with maturity, wisdom, and leadership. The word is often used to describe veteran professionals, seasoned leaders, or historical figures who faced danger without flinching. It carries a slightly formal, admiring tone.

===word: 五里霧中===
Nuance: 五里霧中 describes a state of being completely lost or at a loss, as if surrounded by a thick fog five leagues deep. It is used when one has no idea what to do or which direction to take — when the path forward is utterly unclear. The word conveys confusion and uncertainty, but not necessarily panic; it can be a neutral description of a difficult situation. It is common in business, academia, and personal challenges, and has a slightly formal, literary ring.
Example1: 手がかりがなく、捜査は五里霧中の状態だ。
